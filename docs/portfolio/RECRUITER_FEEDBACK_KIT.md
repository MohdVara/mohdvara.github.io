# Real reviewer feedback kit

Prepared 24 October 2026. Invitations below are drafts, not sent messages. No participants, commitments, reviews or scores have been obtained. Keep responses in a private working copy; the checked-in register intentionally contains no responses.

## Invitation drafts

**Technical recruiter:** “I’m preparing applications for principal full-stack, technical lead and hands-on architecture roles. Would you be willing to review my portfolio and résumé for about three minutes? I’m interested in what role they communicate, what is unclear and what would prevent a conversation. Critical feedback is welcome; declining is fine. Please avoid sharing confidential hiring information. With your permission, I’ll retain anonymized notes for improving these materials.”

**Engineering manager / architect:** “Could you spend about three minutes reviewing my portfolio’s engineering evidence? I’d value your interpretation of ownership, architectural decisions, delivered capabilities and claims you would investigate in an interview. Negative or uncertain reactions are useful. Participation is optional. I’ll ask permission before retaining notes or quoting anything and won’t publish your identity or employer.”

Owner sends invitations individually after choosing actual reviewers. Offer the same production URL and current two-page résumé; record exactly which version was reviewed. Do not lead with intended answers or describe the site as already validated.

## Exercises

**30 seconds:** Start at <https://mohd.paramasvara.online/en/> with no guided tour. Stop after 30 seconds. Without looking back, describe the role and seniority you think this person is pursuing, and one piece of experience you remember. Record the visitor’s own words, uncertainty and whether the time limit was followed.

**Two minutes:** Let the reviewer choose one commercial case, find the résumé and locate a way to contact the engineer. Ask them to think aloud if comfortable. Record the path, anything they could not locate, and which evidence they would verify. Do not “help” them find a destination during the exercise; record assistance if needed. Do not send a message or book a meeting as part of the exercise.

## Five open questions

1. Which engineering role does this portfolio most strongly communicate, and what gave you that impression?
2. What, if anything, makes the experience credible? What reduces your confidence?
3. What information was difficult to find or understand?
4. Which claims would you want clarified in an interview, and why?
5. Would these materials encourage you to request a conversation? Why or why not?

## Response template and consent

Use [the empty register](../../verification/recruiter-feedback-register.json) as a schema reference, not a public collection endpoint. For each actual response record: anonymous review ID; review date; reviewer category and perspective; consent to retain notes; separate consent to quote; page/document URL and version; exercise/time followed; observation; category; severity; authorized quotation or paraphrase; proposed correction; action taken; validation status. Unknown fields stay null; never infer consent from participation.

Explain that participation is voluntary and notes can be corrected or removed on request. Ask whether anonymized notes may be retained and for how long (suggest 90 days). Quotation permission is separate from note permission. Do not request names, employers, email addresses, client information or hiring decisions. Keep any owner-held contact mapping separate and private. Delete declined, withdrawn or expired notes; do not commit raw feedback or identifying details. No form, tracking, recording or outreach automation is installed.

## Initial review plan

Invite a small purposive group of roughly five to seven willing professionals: two technical recruiters familiar with senior engineering recruitment, two engineering managers, and one or two principal engineers/architects. Seek different organization sizes and regional hiring contexts; include someone comfortable with an available non-English locale if possible. These are recruitment targets, not actual participants. Offer desktop/mobile choice and an accessible text/PDF alternative. Capture device, language and review context only when volunteered and useful.

Start with two reviews, fix factual/functional blockers, then use remaining reviews to check those corrections. Record the reviewed version so responses to different versions are not silently combined. A small sample is qualitative evidence, not hiring-market statistics or a conversion-rate estimate.

## Triage and prioritization

Classify each finding as factual error, career inconsistency, navigation/discoverability, readability/accessibility, technical malfunction, ambiguous professional evidence, or subjective visual preference. Mark severity: blocker (cannot complete task or materially false claim), major (likely misinterpretation or substantial friction), minor (recoverable friction), preference (taste without demonstrated task impact).

Address verified factual errors and functional/access barriers first, then recurring comprehension/discovery issues, then isolated preferences. Repetition increases confidence but is not statistical significance; one severe reproducible failure can outrank several preferences. Keep contradictory observations and the owner’s reasoning. Confirm proposed career corrections against the source register, not reviewer assumptions. For each accepted change link the evidence, implementation, and appropriate recheck; record rejected/deferred suggestions with a reason. No positive-rating target is set.
